### How the author writes

- States the problem in plain words before any notation, often as a question: "can a sensor decide on its own when to take the next reading".
- Uses semicolons to join a claim and its reason instead of starting a new sentence: "a fast-changing signal gets short intervals, a flat one gets long intervals".
- Marks the point a reader must not miss with "We stress that ...": "We stress that our goal is narrower".
- Restates a formal step in everyday terms right after it, opening with "Put differently,": "Put differently, each pair of neighbours contributes one linear equation".
- Opens a technical section with "In what follows," followed by the notation: "In what follows, $x(t)$ denotes the concentration".
- Grounds claims in concrete field examples (a truck, a bonfire, a bus stop) rather than adjectives.
- Names limitations directly, numbered "First, ... Second, ...", and says what to do about each.
- Uses British spelling for some words ("neighbouring", "neighbours") and American for others ("minimize", "summarizes"); keep whatever the draft has.

### Slips a pattern cannot catch

None found beyond the watchlist. The samples are consistent in agreement, articles and tense.

### Keep as is

- "We stress that", "Put differently,", "In what follows,", "Concretely,", "In short," and "As a consequence," are deliberate and frequent.
- Semicolons between clauses (about 8 per 1,000 words) are part of the rhythm; do not split them into separate sentences.
- Short closing sentences such as "Any sensor that sees the same events at a stable lag will do."
- "neighbouring" and "neighbours" (British spelling).

### Worked examples of minimal fixes

- Original: "the controller allows to trade energy for coverage with a single parameter"
  Fix: "the controller allows us to trade energy for coverage with a single parameter" (active "allow" needs an object)
- Original: "Despite of the longer life, it detects more peaks"
  Fix: "Despite the longer life, it detects more peaks" ("despite" takes no "of")
- Original: "takes less samples than the fixed schedule by a factor of six"
  Fix: "takes fewer samples than the fixed schedule by a factor of six" (countable noun)
- Not an error, leave it: "This leaves a sparser graph, but a more reliable one." The short contrast sentence is the author's habit.
